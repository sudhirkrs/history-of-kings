# Ep 20 — Napoleon: From Corsican Officer to Emperor of the French

## 1. HOOK (0:00–0:50)
**NARRATION**
On 2 December 1804, in Notre-Dame cathedral in Paris, the Pope was about to place a crown on the head of a man from a minor family on the island of Corsica. At the last moment, the man took the crown from the Pope's hands and crowned himself. Then he crowned his wife.
[VISUAL: Notre-Dame, Pope, a man taking the crown, painting by David]
Eleven years earlier he had been an unknown artillery captain. Ten years later he would be exiled on Elba. This is Napoleon Bonaparte: a general who won more battles than almost anyone in history, a ruler who wrote laws still used today, and a man whose ambition led to wars that killed millions. Let us follow his life, step by step.
[VISUAL: Napoleon portrait, map of Europe in 1812]

## 2. A CORSICAN BOY (0:50–3:00)
**NARRATION**
Napoleone di Buonaparte was born on 15 August 1769 in Ajaccio, Corsica, a year after France bought the island from Genoa. His family was of minor nobility. His father, Carlo, had fought for Corsican independence, then accepted French rule. At nine, Napoleon was sent to France to attend a military school at Brienne, and then in 1784 to the Ecole Militaire in Paris. He was a small, serious, poor boy with a heavy Corsican accent, teased by his classmates. He read history, geography and mathematics.
[VISUAL: Ajaccio, Brienne school, young Napoleon alone]
In 1785 he became a second lieutenant of artillery. He was sixteen. He spent the next years reading, writing, and moving between France and Corsica. When the Revolution broke out in 1789, he welcomed it, but his career depended on events.

## 3. THE REVOLUTION'S GENERAL (3:00–6:00)
**NARRATION**
In 1793, Napoleon's family was forced to leave Corsica after a split with the Corsican leader Pasquale Paoli. That same year, royalists in the port of Toulon handed it to the British fleet. Captain Bonaparte, in charge of the artillery of the besieging army, devised a plan to take a hill that commanded the harbour. It worked. Toulon fell in December 1793. At twenty-four, he was promoted to brigadier general.
[VISUAL: siege of Toulon, hill battery]
After the fall of Robespierre in 1794, he was briefly imprisoned. In October 1795, he saved the government, the Directory, from a royalist mob in Paris. He fired cannon on the crowd at the church of Saint-Roch, and ended the uprising. He was rewarded with command of the Army of the Interior, and later of the Army of Italy.
In March 1796 he married Josephine de Beauharnais, a widow six years older than him. Two days later he left for Italy.
[VISUAL: 13 Vendemiaire, cannon on Paris streets, Josephine]
In the Italian campaign of 1796 and 1797, he took a ragged, unpaid army and defeated the Austrians and their allies in a series of battles at Lodi, Arcole and Rivoli. He used speed, concentration of force and surprise. He sent home vast amounts of looted art. The Treaty of Campo Formio in 1797 made France the master of Italy. He became a national hero.
[VISUAL: Italian campaign map, Arcole bridge]

## 4. EGYPT AND THE COUP (6:00–8:30)
**NARRATION**
In 1798, the Directory sent him to Egypt, hoping to threaten British trade with India. He took 35,000 men and 150 scholars, who began the scientific study of Egypt. He won at the Battle of the Pyramids in July. But on 1 August, Admiral Nelson destroyed the French fleet at the Nile. Napoleon's army was stranded.
[VISUAL: Battle of the Pyramids, Nile, Nelson]
The scholars published the Description of Egypt, and found the Rosetta Stone, which later allowed Champollion to decipher hieroglyphs. But the campaign itself failed. In 1799, Napoleon secretly left the army and sailed for France.
He arrived to find the Directory in crisis. On 9 November 1799, the 18 Brumaire in the revolutionary calendar, he and his allies carried out a coup d'etat. After a messy day, in which he was jostled by deputies, soldiers cleared the legislature. A new government, the Consulate, was set up, with three consuls. Napoleon was First Consul. Within a few weeks, a new constitution gave him almost all power.
[VISUAL: Brumaire coup, soldiers in the chamber]

## 5. THE FIRST CONSUL (8:30–12:00)
**NARRATION**
Napoleon was thirty. In 1800, he crossed the Alps to surprise the Austrians in Italy, and won the battle of Marengo on 14 June. In 1801 the Peace of Luneville ended the war on the continent, and the Peace of Amiens in 1802 ended the war with Britain, briefly.
[VISUAL: crossing the Alps, Marengo]
He then rebuilt France. He set up the Banque de France in 1800. He created the prefects, officials in each department, to enforce the central government's orders. He founded lycees, state secondary schools. He created the Legion of Honour in 1802, to reward merit, civil and military.
In 1801, he signed a Concordat with the Pope, which restored peace between the French state and the Catholic Church. Priests were paid by the state, but the Church accepted the sale of its lands in the Revolution.
[VISUAL: banknote, lycee, Legion of Honour medal]
His greatest peacetime achievement is the Civil Code, finished in 1804 and later called the Napoleonic Code. It guaranteed equality before the law, the right to property, and freedom of religion, and abolished feudal privileges. It was also conservative for women: wives were legally subordinate to their husbands. It spread across Europe with his armies and influenced law in many countries, from Latin America to Louisiana.
[VISUAL: Code civil book, map of its spread]
He also reintroduced slavery in the French colonies in 1802, and sent an army to retake Saint-Domingue, which failed, and led to the independence of Haiti in 1804. Historians regard this as a dark side of his rule.

## 6. EMPEROR AND CONQUEROR (12:00–15:30)
**NARRATION**
In 1802 he became First Consul for life. On 18 May 1804, the Senate proclaimed him Emperor of the French. A referendum approved it by a huge majority, though such votes were not free. On 2 December came the coronation.
[VISUAL: Senate, coronation]
War with Britain resumed in 1803. Napoleon planned to invade England, but his fleet was destroyed by Nelson at Trafalgar on 21 October 1805. So he turned east. His Grande Armee marched across Europe, and on 2 December 1805, exactly one year after his coronation, he won his masterpiece at Austerlitz, against Austria and Russia. He deliberately weakened his right flank to lure the allies, then broke their centre. The Holy Roman Empire was dissolved in 1806.
[VISUAL: Trafalgar, Austerlitz]
Next came Prussia, crushed at Jena and Auerstedt in 1806, and Russia, fought to a bloody draw at Eylau and beaten at Friedland in 1807. By the Treaty of Tilsit, Napoleon was master of Europe. He put his brothers on thrones: Joseph in Naples and then Spain, Louis in Holland, Jerome in Westphalia.
Since Britain was unbeatable at sea, he tried economic war. The Continental System of 1806 banned British goods from continental ports. It hurt Britain, but also Europe, and Napoleon had to enforce it by invading Portugal and Spain, and by pressuring Russia.
[VISUAL: map of Napoleonic Europe, Continental System]

## 7. THE FALL (15:30–19:00)
**NARRATION**
In 1808, Napoleon put his brother Joseph on the throne of Spain. The Spanish people rose in revolt, and the British sent an army under Wellington. The Peninsular War, which Napoleon later called the Spanish ulcer, drained French strength for six years.
[VISUAL: Spanish guerrilla, Goya's Third of May]
Then, in 1812, he invaded Russia, with the largest army Europe had seen, around 600,000 men from many nations. The Russians retreated and burned the land. The Battle of Borodino on 7 September was bloody and inconclusive. He entered Moscow on 14 September, but fires broke out, perhaps set by the Russians themselves, and Tsar Alexander did not surrender. In October, Napoleon began the retreat. Winter, hunger and disease finished the Grande Armee. Of the roughly 600,000 men who crossed the border, most were killed, captured or deserted, and only a small fraction came home in fighting shape. Estimates of the losses vary widely.
[VISUAL: Borodino, burning Moscow, retreating army in snow]
Europe rose against him. At Leipzig in October 1813, the Battle of the Nations, he was defeated by Austria, Prussia, Russia and Sweden. In March 1814, allied armies entered Paris. Napoleon abdicated on 6 April and was exiled to the island of Elba, in the Mediterranean.
On 1 March 1815, he escaped from Elba with about 1,000 men, landed in France, and marched north. Soldiers sent to arrest him joined him instead. He entered Paris on 20 March. This is the Hundred Days.
[VISUAL: Elba escape, soldiers joining him]
The allies declared him an outlaw. On 18 June 1815, at Waterloo in Belgium, Napoleon met the Duke of Wellington's British and allied army, and the Prussians under Blucher. After a day of bitter fighting, the arrival of the Prussians on his right flank decided the battle. His last army was destroyed. He abdicated again on 22 June, and surrendered to the British.
[VISUAL: Waterloo, Prussian arrival]

## 8. ST HELENA AND LEGACY (19:00–21:00)
**NARRATION**
The British exiled him to St Helena, a remote island in the South Atlantic, 2,000 kilometres from Africa. He lived there for six years, dictating his memoirs, arguing with the governor Hudson Lowe and shaping his own legend. He died on 5 May 1821, aged fifty-one, most likely of stomach cancer. A 2021 study supported that diagnosis, and the arsenic-poisoning theory has been rejected by most scholars. His remains were returned to Paris in 1840 and rest in Les Invalides.
[VISUAL: St Helena, tomb at Les Invalides]
What did he leave? The Napoleonic Code. A modern, centralised state. Ideas of nationalism and liberalism, spread unintentionally by his conquests. But also a death toll of perhaps three to six million people in the Napoleonic Wars, and a continent in ruins.
Historians still debate. Was he a child of the Revolution or its betrayer? A military genius or a gambler who overreached? A modernizer or a dictator? In France he is still admired by many. In others, especially Haiti, Spain and Russia, he is remembered differently.
[VISUAL: two views, hero and tyrant]
