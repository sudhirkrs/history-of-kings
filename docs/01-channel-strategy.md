# Channel Strategy

## Positioning
"Clear, honest, story-driven biographies of the world's kings — what happened, how we know, and why it mattered."
Differentiator: **authenticity**. Every episode separates *what sources prove* from *what legend says*.

## Audience
- Primary: English-speaking viewers in India (18–45) curious about Indian history, plus the diaspora.
- Secondary: global history fans once the series reaches the Mongols, Russia, Europe, China.
- Consider a Hindi version later (same scripts, translated) — doubles reach in India.

## Format
- Length: 15–20 min (never pad; 14 min is fine). Shorts (45–60 s) cut from each episode for discovery.
- Voice: calm narrator, simple words, short sentences, one idea per sentence.
- Visuals: maps (animated routes/borders), paintings, coins, inscriptions, monuments, timeline graphics, light 2D motion.
- Structure: hook → context → rise → peak → fall → legacy → "what historians still debate".

## Cadence
- Launch with 3 videos in week one (Ep1–3) so new viewers can binge; then 1 long video/week + 2–3 Shorts.
- Batch: script 4 episodes ahead so a bad week never breaks the schedule.

## Series arc (see roadmap)
1. India (Season 1) → 2. Mongols/Central Asia → 3. Russia → 4. Europe → 5. China → later: Middle East, Africa, Southeast Asia.
Playlists per season; "Kings of ..." naming for consistency.

## Growth levers
- Title + thumbnail promise one clear question ("Why did Ashoka give up war?").
- First 30 seconds: state the hook, no long intros/logos.
- Pin a comment asking which king next; reply to comments the first 2 hours.
- Cross-link episodes with end screens ("Ashoka's grandfather: Chandragupta →").
- Consistent look: fixed fonts, colours, thumbnail template.

## Monetisation (YouTube Partner Programme)
Requires 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 public watch hours (12 months) or 10M Shorts views (90 days). Plan on 3–6 months of steady posting.

## Rules to protect the channel
- Original script and narration, transformative visuals (avoid "reused/mass-produced content" demonetisation).
- Disclose AI voice/imagery where YouTube's altered-or-synthetic content setting requires it.
- Use only public-domain / properly licensed images (Wikimedia Commons, museum open-access) with credits in description.
- Be respectful on communal/caste/religion topics; present historiography neutrally.
